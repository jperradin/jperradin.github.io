About

My research sits at the interface of statistical physics, computational materials science, and numerical methods. I am broadly interested in the physics of disordered and complex systems, with a focus on structural transitions, percolation phenomena, and the development of computational tools to characterise them.

Current work

As a postdoctoral researcher at Laboratoire Gulliver (ESPCI Paris PSL), working with L. Berthier, I benchmark Monte Carlo algorithms applied to liquid and supercooled liquid silica.

Doctoral thesis

My Ph.D. (Université de Montpellier, defended November 2025) studied polyamorphism in vitreous silica under pressure: treating amorphous–amorphous transitions as percolation and critical phenomena, and connecting them to the mechanical anomalies of compressed glass. This relies on classical and ab initio molecular dynamics, neural-network interatomic potentials (HDNNP), metadynamics, and cluster analysis, supported by electronic-structure methods (DFT, DFTB). [thesis pdf]

Much of the methodology behind this work is released as open-source software, including Nexus-CAT, a framework for long-range structural descriptors in glassy materials built from percolation theory.
