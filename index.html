About & Research

A short statement of what I work on and why.

My research sits at the interface of statistical physics, computational materials science, and numerical methods. I am broadly interested in the physics of disordered and complex systems, with a focus on structural transitions, percolation phenomena, and the development of computational tools to characterise them.

My doctoral work studied polyamorphism in vitreous silica under pressure: treating amorphous–amorphous transitions as percolation and critical phenomena, and connecting them to the mechanical anomalies of compressed glass. This relies on classical and ab initio molecular dynamics, neural-network interatomic potentials (HDNNP), metadynamics, and cluster analysis, supported by electronic-structure methods (DFT, DFTB).

I care about reproducible, open computational work. Much of the methodology behind my papers is released as software — including Nexus-CAT, a framework for long-range structural descriptors in glassy materials built from percolation theory. In the long term I aim for an academic position combining research with teaching. Outside the lab, you'll usually find me with a guitar, on a bike, or cooking.
